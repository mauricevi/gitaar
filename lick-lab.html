How it works

  1. Set up first: the chord you play the lick over, the position (where your index finger sits), a slow tempo and the rhythm grid. Press Record: one bar of clicks, then play. Notes in the count-in bar become pickups.
  2. Check the fingering. The app puts every note on the string that fits your position best. Click a note and press Other string to move it; it stays there. Notes can also be moved earlier or later, deleted, or added by clicking the neck.
  3. Pick a chord. The whole shape slides up or down the neck, so your fingers do exactly the same thing. Auto uses Pat Martino’s minor conversion: a minor lick over a 7 chord starts from its 5 (Am over D7 = D9), over maj7 from its 6 (Am over Cmaj7), over m7♭5 from its ♭3 (Dm over Bm7♭5). Play from lets you choose any other degree.
  4. Practise the lick over a progression with piano, bass and click. Turn on a few BPM per round to build speed.

First time? Press Calibrate timing and play a short muted note on each of the last eight clicks: the app measures the delay of your audio interface, so your notes land on the grid. Keys: R record · Space play · Esc stop · ← → select a note · Shift ← → move it · S other string · Delete · A add notes · Ctrl Z undo.
