Lick Lab

? Library lists all your licks. Click one to open it; Esc in a lick brings you back. Record a lick once, then play it with the same fingering over every chord. 1 · Set up first: the lick’s chords (Harmony, e.g. Dm7 G7 | Cmaj7 or one chord), the position (where your index finger sits) and a slow tempo; grid and length are in the side panel. Press Record: one bar of clicks, then play (the band plays your chords along if Band while recording is on). Notes in the count-in bar become pickups. 2 · Check the fingering. Every note goes on the string that fits your position best. Click a note and press Other string to move it; it stays there. Notes can also move earlier or later, be deleted, or be added by clicking the neck. String ↓ / ↑ moves the whole shape one string lower or higher; Octave ↓ / ↑ moves it 12 frets (in another key or over another chord only there). 3 · Own chords: pick a Key. The whole shape slides along the neck with the same fingering, the chords follow. Save in this key makes it the recorded key. ▶ 12 keys: per key the band plays it with the lick (listen), then without (your turn); finished keys light up in the ring. Over a progression (one-chord licks): the shape slides to each chord. Auto uses Pat Martino’s minor conversion: a minor lick over a 7 chord starts from its 5 (Am over D7 = D9), over maj7 from its 6 (Am over Cmaj7), over m7♭5 from its ♭3 (Dm over Bm7♭5). Play from picks any other degree. 4 · Practise with guitar chords, bass and click. In the side panel you can add a few BPM per round (in 12 keys: per key). First time? Press Calibrate timing (side panel) and play a short muted note on each of the last eight clicks, so your notes land on the grid.

?The chords the lick goes over, at most 16. | is a bar line: chords in one bar share it (two = 2 beats each, four = a beat each); a lone chord fills its bar. Without | every chord gets a bar. The last chord lasts until the lick ends.
Examples: Am7 · Dm7 G7 | Cmaj7 · Bm7b5 E7 | Am6
BPM?The recording tempo. Play slowly: the app keeps the rhythm, and you practise faster with the tempo in the play bar.
