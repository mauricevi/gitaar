How to read it. Play one string and let it ring. The bands compare your string with a perfect reference tone of the target note. Moving right: too high, tune down. Moving left: too low, tune up. Standing still: in tune. The faster the pattern moves, the further off you are.

The three bands show the fundamental, the 2nd and the 4th harmonic. The lower bands move faster, so they show the smallest errors. Right after you pick, the note is a little sharp and the pattern drifts; read it once the string has settled.

Why a strobe?

  • Precise. A needle tuner is accurate to about ±2–3 cents; a strobe shows errors far below one cent.
  • Calm. No jittery needle and no “in tune” light that guesses for you: your eye reads the motion.
  • Shows the whole note. You see the sharp attack and how the pitch settles, which makes it the tool of choice for setting intonation.

Auto picks the nearest note. Pick a string to lock the target, handy for intonation: compare the open string with the 12th fret.
